# Prompt: a demo video with your face, edited from the webcam recording

For a recording made with **Record my camera** on. Give the AI `bundle.json`, `transcript.json`, the `frames/` folder, the scenario file and `camera.webm`. Not `screen.webm`, not `voice.webm`. This prompt needs a video tool behind the AI, since the camera file is video. Fill the brackets, then paste. (For a video with no one on camera and a synthesized voice, use `demo-video-faceless.md`.)

---

Edit a polished product demo out of the presenter's own webcam recording. The presenter's voice from `camera.webm` is the soundtrack: there is no script to write and no voice to synthesize. The screen is shown as stills brought to life with camera moves, a cursor, outlines and captions; the presenter's face is overlaid from the same clip. Show only what the frames show; cut, never invent.

**Product:** [ComicInk, an AI comic book creator]. **Audience:** [new users]. **Length:** [60–90 s]. **Output:** 1920×1080, 30 fps MP4, the presenter's own voice, burned-in captions.

## The files

All times are milliseconds on one clock starting at `bundle.json.started_at`.

- `camera.webm`: the presenter, 1280×720, face and voice, starting `bundle.json.camera.offset_ms` after the clock's zero. Its audio is the narration and the only soundtrack; its picture is the face overlay. Every cut you make applies to picture and sound together, so they stay in sync by construction.
- `transcript.json`: `text` (the narration, punctuated), `words[]` with `start_ms`/`end_ms` on the shared clock (subtract `camera.offset_ms` to find a word inside `camera.webm`), `steps[]` sliced per step (split at clicks, so take sentence boundaries from `text`). This is how you find what to cut without listening twice.
- `frames/<time>-step<nn>-<action|settled>.jpg`: the screen at the instant of each click (`action`) and once the page had reacted (`settled`), plus `start` and `stop`. These are the screen footage. They are 2× the viewport, so a 2× punch-in is still full resolution.
- `bundle.json`: `steps[]` is every action — `do`/`target` (what was clicked or typed), `at_ms`, `settled_at_ms`, `click.x`/`y` (the click) and `click.box` (the clicked control's rectangle: top-left at `box.x`,`box.y`, bottom-right at `box.x+box.width`,`box.y+box.height`), both in viewport pixels at the moment of the click; multiply by `screen.width / viewport.width` (2 here) for frame pixels. `dropped[]` is clicks on things that are not controls (plain text, canvas, the page background); each has `x`/`y` where it landed and a `frames/<time>-dropped.jpg` still of that moment, but no `target` or `box`.
- `pointer.json` (optional, do not read unless you need it): the raw cursor path, `t`/`x`/`y`, thinned to its corners and pauses. Context only; see Cursor.
- `../<scenario>.json`: the clean step list with a note per step; use it as the chapter list.

## Do this

1. **Cut list.** Using `words[]`, mark every stretch to remove from `camera.webm`: every "um", "uh", "ah", "er", "hmm" and "like" used as a pause, filler phrases ("okay, so", "all right", "let's wait on that", "basically", "you know"), false starts, repeats, and every silence or wait longer than about 700 ms (the generation wait at the end becomes 2–3 s). The transcript does not always write the hesitations down, so also listen through the clip once, or run a silence and disfluency pass on its audio, and cut what the words list missed. Cut each hesitation tightly, from the end of the word before it to the start of the word after it, with a 40–80 ms audio crossfade so the join is inaudible; if the camera jumps visibly at a tight cut, hide the join under a 150 ms crossfade of the face overlay rather than keeping the hesitation. Cut sentences at boundaries from `text`, never mid-word; keep every claim the presenter makes. The cut clip sets the timeline and must land inside the length target; say how long it is. Output the cut list as a table: in/out on the shared clock, what was removed and why.
2. **Scenes.** The presenter's words decide what is on screen: while they talk about a step, show that step's `action` frame, then cut or crossfade to its `settled` frame once the "click" lands. Map each step's `at_ms` through the cut list to place it on the new timeline. Merge steps the presenter covers in one breath; a step they never mention gets a 1-second beat or is dropped. The wait at the end is the `stop` frame with a progress caption.
3. **Camera and framing.** Treat every still as one layer under one continuous virtual camera. Fix the framing once: the stills are all the same size, so compute one scale and offset that makes a still cover the 1920×1080 frame (crop a little off the top and bottom; never letterbox, never black bars at the sides) and apply it to every still identically. Slow drift on every still so nothing is static. Where a line points at something, punch in 1.6–2× on `click.box` over 300–500 ms, ease in and out, hold, ease out on the `settled` frame. Not on every click. Motion blur on moves only.
4. **Continuity.** The camera never resets at a cut. The scale and position on the last frame of one still are the scale and position on the first frame of the next, whether the cut is action to settled or scene to scene; swap the image under the held camera (a hard cut, or a 150–250 ms crossfade), and only then, if the next line needs it, start the next move. Never zoom out and back in across a cut, never make a scale change smaller than 5% (it reads as a jerk, not a move), and hold at least 500 ms between moves. Before delivering, step through every cut frame by frame: the page's edges must sit in the same place on both sides of it.
5. **Cursor.** Draw a synthetic cursor; never replay the raw `pointer.json` path, which is jittery and wanders. Show it only when it has a job: for each click, fade it in where it was last seen (or just off-screen), move it to `click.x`/`y` along one smooth eased curve over 400–700 ms, arrive a beat before the click, ripple, then fade it out within about half a second of the result appearing. Hidden between clicks. No hovering, hesitation or back-and-forth.
6. **Outline.** As the cursor arrives, draw a red outline (2–3 px, 4 px outside `click.box`, slightly rounded) around the clicked control on the `action` frame; hold it through the click and the cut to the `settled` frame, then fade it with the cursor. One at a time, only on clicks the presenter mentions, never on `dropped[]` clicks (they have a point and a still, but no box: show the cursor arriving there, no outline).
7. **Face.** Overlay the cut `camera.webm` in the bottom-right corner, about a fifth of the frame wide, rounded corners, a soft shadow. Show it during the opening and whenever the presenter is talking about themselves, the idea or the product in general; shrink or hide it during zooms and whenever it would cover the control being clicked or a caption. Every cut in the overlay is a cut you already made in step 1, so the lips and the sound always match.
8. **Sound.** The presenter's audio, levelled to about −16 LUFS, with light noise reduction and a short fade at every cut so no edit clicks. No synthesized voice. A quiet music bed at most, ducked under speech.
9. **Captions.** From the presenter's actual words, one short line at a time (3–8 words), lower third, cleaned of the filler you cut and nothing else. **Intro** title card (2–3 s) and **outro** with the URL (3 s).
10. **Check.** No cut moves the page's edges; no \"um\", \"uh\" or long pause is left in the audio; Every frame shown matches what the presenter is saying over it; every step in order; within length; no cut mid-word; the face overlay never covers a caption or the clicked control; nothing from the removed stretches is audible.

Deliver the cut-list table, a scene table (scene, frames used, timeline in/out, zoom target, face shown or hidden, caption), and the video.
